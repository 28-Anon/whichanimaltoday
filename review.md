## Q112045 — Japanese giant salamander

![Japanese giant salamander](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Japanese_giant_salamander_in_Tottori_Prefecture,_Japan.jpg?width=600)

**Clues**
1. I never leave the water—dry land is someone else's problem, not mine.
2. Stretch me out and I could hit five feet, though I'd rather not be measured, thank you.
3. You'll find me tucked into rivers across western Honshu, with a few cousins hiding out on Shikoku and northern Kyushu—bronze medal for size in my family, just behind two very similar relatives across the water.

**Fun facts:** This amphibian can grow up to 5 feet (1.5 m) long, making it the third-largest salamander species on Earth. It's edged out only by its two closest relatives, the Chinese giant salamander and the South China giant salamander.

## Q61312 — Chinese giant salamander

![Chinese giant salamander](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Velemlok_čínský_zoo_praha_1.jpg?width=600)

**Clues**
1. I'm not just big for my family—I'm one of the largest amphibians on the entire planet, and I never bother stepping onto dry land.
2. Home is a rocky mountain stream or lake, though these days I'm rarer to find there than on a dinner plate or in a medicine cabinet.
3. I'm the world's largest living amphibian, farmed by the thousands across central China's Yangtze basin—surely you've guessed what I am by now.

**Fun facts:** Despite being extensively farmed, many of the animals on those farms were actually caught in the wild rather than bred there. In 2008 it was named one of the top-10 'focal species' by the Evolutionarily Distinct and Globally Endangered project, recognizing just how ancient and irreplaceable its lineage is.
