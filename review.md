## Q82037 — Okapi

![Okapi](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Okapi_and_son.jpg?width=600)

**Clues**
1. People keep mistaking my legs for a zebra's, but that's just a stylish disguise hiding a much taller family secret.
2. I keep to the dense, hard-to-reach forests of the northeastern Congo, and I'm not exactly the type to pose for a group photo.
3. My closest living relative has a wildly stretched-out neck, and together we're the only two branches left on our whole family tree.

**Fun facts:** Despite sporting zebra-like stripes on its legs and rear, this animal is actually the giraffe's nearest living relative, not any horse-striped cousin. It's the only species in its entire genus, and recent genetic sleuthing suggests a hidden population may live southwest of the Congo River, beyond its known range.

## Q126683 — Mongolian Saiga

![Mongolian Saiga](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Saiga_tatarica,_Bokeiorda_Reserve,_Kazakhstan_DIL00119.jpg?width=600)

**Clues**
1. I'm a ghost of the Ice Age, once loping across a steppe so vast it stretched from Britain all the way to Beringia.
2. My relatives used to roam from the foothills of the Carpathians to the deserts of Dzungaria, but most of those old haunts don't have me anymore.
3. These days you'll only find my particular kind wandering the western reaches of one landlocked Asian country.

**Fun facts:** During the Pleistocene, animals of this kind roamed the mammoth steppe across an enormous range stretching from the British Isles to Beringia. Today the population is so fragmented that it has vanished entirely from Romania, Ukraine, Moldova, China, and southwestern Mongolia, surviving as its own distinct subspecies only in the western part of that last country.

## Q186778 — Aye-aye

![Aye-aye](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Aye-aye_(Daubentonia_madagascariensis),_Tsimbazaza_Zoo,_Madagascar_(3897947810).jpg?width=600)

**Clues**
1. I'm a primate with a secret: my teeth never stop growing, so I'm basically gnawing my way through eternity.
2. I've got one wonderfully weird, extra-skinny finger that I use like a built-in fork for grubs hiding in tree bark.
3. I'm a nocturnal lemur from Madagascar, famous for my spindly middle finger and rodent-like grin.

**Fun facts:** The aye-aye's teeth grow continuously throughout its life, much like a rodent's, even though it's a primate. Its unusually thin middle finger acts like a built-in tool, letting it tap on wood to find hollows and then hook out grubs and larvae.

## Q276290 — Naked mole rat

![Naked mole rat](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Special:FilePath/Nacktmull.jpg?width=600)

**Clues**
1. I burrow through sun-baked earth in the Horn of Africa, keeping well away from anyone above ground.
2. My closest kin are known as blesmols, yet I'm the only member of my very own genus — no family reunions for me.
3. I'm nearly hairless, wrinkled, and live in huge underground colonies stretching from Somali regions into Kenya — my odd name gives it away if you think about it.

**Fun facts:** This creature is the sole living species in its entire genus, Heterocephalus. It's found only in a narrow range across the Horn of Africa, including Somali regions and parts of Kenya.
